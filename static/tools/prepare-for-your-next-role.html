A 10-minute analysis

Prepare for Your Next Role

You were competent, and they still chose someone else. That is rarely one thing — timing, circumstances and who happened to be in the room all played their part. It is also rarely as personal as it feels at 11pm. Whether the next one comes to you or not, you can be ready for it. About ten minutes.

Step 1 · where you are

What you have already built

Start here, and be generous with yourself. You did not get this far by accident. Whatever happened with that promotion, none of it erases what follows — and much of it was never yours alone to carry.

Three things you have delivered, built or carried. You might notice you want to qualify them — leave the qualifiers out. This is simply the ground you are standing on.

Step 2 · where you want to go

The role you are actually aiming for

You may already know what it is. Naming it as concretely as you can tends to make it easier to ask for, when the moment comes.

Step 3 · the gap

What is in the way — and what is missing

Two different things, and you may find one easier to write than the other. An obstacle stands in front of you. What is missing is what you do not yet have — a skill, a sponsor, a piece of visibility. Some of these are yours to move. Some are not, and noticing which is which is most of the work.

Step 4 · what you need

What would actually help

None of this is yours to manage alone. You might consider who could tell you honestly how you come across, or who already has the ear of the people deciding.

Step 5 · the conversation

How you open it, next time

The next role is rarely won in the interview. More often it is won in a conversation some months earlier, with the person who decides. You may find it easier to draft that opening here, quietly, before you need it.

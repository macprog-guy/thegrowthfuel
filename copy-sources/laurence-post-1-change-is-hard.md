Why do smart, capable people stay in situations that aren't good for them?
Not because they lack a survival instinct.
But because:

When they come home after a day full of emergencies and interruptions, change
feels like just one more energy drain they don't have capacity for.
So they watch Netflix.
Passive watching feels easier (at first) than having a deep conversation.

We've all done that at some point.

OK, but if it's not working, why do we keep tolerating it?

Three main reasons:

1. Inertia
"If it worked before, it should work again, right?"
Even if the situation has changed, we cling to the same playbook.

2. Timing
"Next month will be better."
This month feels too chaotic, but next month? That's when things will finally
calm down. We keep waiting for the perfect time to make a change, but that time
rarely comes.

3. Change requires effort.
Improvement means to review and change some part of our behaviour.
And behaviour change means discomfort and intentional action.

The good part:
You don't have to become someone new.
You just have to add to who you already are.
Like any athlete. You keep your strengths. You build new ones.

And the benefits?
Empowerment.
Deeper connections.
A more peaceful life.
That's when the initial discomfort starts to feel worth it.

So, what's one change you've been putting off that might be worth revisiting?

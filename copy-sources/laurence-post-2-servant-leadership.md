→ If you value servant leadership but wonder what might need tweaking, this
post is for you.
Because I've seen too many leaders fall into this trap.

Often, it starts with the best intentions—a desire to serve, to support, to
lead with heart.

But sometimes, that same instinct quietly undermines the very leadership we're
trying to embody.

That's why servant leadership can backfire (even for great leaders).

Servant leadership is often praised as the gold standard.
But what if I told you it can quietly sabotage even the most well-meaning
leaders?

Here's what's beneath the surface, and how leaders can navigate it more
effectively:

1. Servant leadership means serving your team, not surrendering to it.
Being kind doesn't mean avoiding tough calls. Misalignments and conflict are
part of the job. Leaders must open communication, but also close the loop with
clear decisions.

2. Empathy without authority leads to confusion.
Seeking to be liked or validated can feel like servant leadership, especially
when confidence dips.
→ Leadership isn't about being liked. No one is, all the time.
It's about accepting that some people may dislike you, or your decisions, as
long as you're serving the collective team and the organisation, and staying
true to the values and goals you've committed to.

3. Clarity builds trust.
Support doesn't always mean saying yes.
→ Sometimes, the most empowering thing a leader can offer is clear direction.
The team needs you to stand firm, own your decisions, and communicate clearly.

4. You can evolve without losing who you are.
→ After 40, we're called to develop new qualities - as Carl Jung noted 100
years ago. If we don't, we risk becoming a caricature of ourselves...
... Who wants that?

→ Adding a different skillset doesn't mean abandoning your strengths.
It means adding to them, expanding your leadership range.
→ Leadership isn't about rejection. It's about integration.
→ Stay true to who you are, and create space to grow intentionally.

Empathy is powerful. Misused, it's exhausting.
